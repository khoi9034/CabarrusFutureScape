# CFS Investment Acquisition-Review Presentation

## Slide 1: Investment Question and Strategy

Main message: Identify whether a large Cabarrus County development-land candidate should advance from screening into acquisition pricing.

Key evidence: 100-acre minimum, residential finished-lot strategy, utility-proxy preference, manageable mapped constraints, and professional diligence requirements.

Suggested visual: Strategy and decision-gate card.

Speaker notes: The case tests site-selection discipline and underwriting discipline. It is not a purchase recommendation.

## Slide 2: Market and Study-Area Context

Main message: Cabarrus County has enough growth and housing-market context to justify screening, but public market evidence does not prove parcel-level financial feasibility.

Key evidence: Charlotte/Cabarrus home-price context, ACS context, development momentum, and public planning references.

Suggested visual: Study-area context with benchmark callouts.

Speaker notes: Finished-home prices and public planning references are indirect benchmarks, not finished-lot comps or entitlements.

## Slide 3: Screening Criteria and Methodology

Main message: The 100-point analyst screen translates the acquisition strategy into repeatable parcel review.

Key evidence: Planning 20, market 20, transportation 15, utility proxy 15, environmental 15, site fit 10, evidence confidence 5.

Suggested visual: Score rubric.

Speaker notes: The score is not Model Lab, a probability, an appraisal, or a purchase instruction.

## Slide 4: Countywide Candidate Funnel

Main message: The funnel narrowed 110,017 parcel records to 3 comparison candidates.

Key evidence: 241 acreage-qualified and evidence-ready parcels; 62 initial-screen pass; 10 manual review; 3 final shortlist.

Suggested visual: Funnel chart.

Speaker notes: The shortlist is preserved. No score changes are proposed.

## Slide 5: Three-Property Comparison

Main message: The priority candidate has the best physical/planning screen, while the secondary and deferred candidates show why acreage alone is insufficient.

Key evidence: CFS-PARCEL-0149758869 score 89; CFS-PARCEL-0149760035 score 77; CFS-PARCEL-0149777275 score 36.

## Slide 6: Priority-Property Deep Dive

Main message: CFS-PARCEL-0149758869 is the strongest site-screening candidate but still has major verification gaps.

Key evidence: 489.43 gross acres, 392.11 preliminary developable acres, adjacent sewer-proximity proxy, moderate mapped constraints, and high screening data confidence.

Suggested visual: Evidence matrix and developable-area bridge.

Speaker notes: Utility proximity is a screening proxy and does not confirm service availability or capacity.

## Slide 7: Underwriting and Sensitivity

Main message: The site screened strongly but does not currently support a positive land basis.

Key evidence: Downside residual -$110.20M, base -$64.34M, upside -$14.25M after selling/carry. Break-even lot values are above current benchmarks.

Suggested visual: Scenario comparison and value-versus-cost sensitivity.

Speaker notes: The model preserves the negative result. No assumptions are increased to force viability.

## Slide 8: Recommendation and Due-Diligence Plan

Main message: Targeted diligence only; do not advance to acquisition pricing yet.

Key evidence: Needed evidence includes finished-lot comps, civil cost, utility capacity, off-site scope, entitlement density, legal access, title/easements, environmental/geotechnical review, and phasing.

Suggested visual: Decision matrix and diligence checklist.

Speaker notes: Screening priority does not equal financial feasibility. Reconsider only if verified assumptions materially improve.
