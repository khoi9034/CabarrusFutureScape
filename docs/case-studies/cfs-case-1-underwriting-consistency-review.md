# CASE-1 Underwriting Consistency Review

CASE-3A.2 is a diagnostic review only. It does not create a workbook, final residual land value, valuation, appraisal, investment advice, asking price, or acquisition recommendation.

## Executive Finding

The current downside, base, and upside assumptions are not internally viable for residual land-value modeling. Each scenario produces a negative pre-land residual before selling and carry costs. After adding the proposed selling and carry conventions, each scenario becomes more negative.

Do not raise finished-lot values or cut costs merely to force a positive answer. The correct next step is user review plus professional market, civil, utility, transportation, entitlement, and finance input.

## Inconsistencies Identified

Finished-lot value is derived from indirect evidence, while horizontal development cost is an analyst benchmark without a civil estimate. The two ranges are not compatible: proposed per-lot revenue does not cover internal horizontal cost, fixed off-site costs, soft costs, contingency, margin, selling, and carry.

The prior "other off-site or pump-station allowance" can overlap with the sewer/special-utility allowance. It should stay blank unless a distinct facility is identified.

## Benchmark-Definition Review

NAHB finished-lot share is useful for lot-value context, not a direct local finished-lot comp. NAHB lot-value data is national/South Atlantic context, not a Cabarrus subdivision-lot sale. Canopy, Redfin, and FRED home-price data are indirect home-market benchmarks, not lot values. Concord and WSACC fee schedules are fee references, not utility capacity confirmations or extension estimates.

Do not combine benchmarks from different definitions without adjustment.

## Horizontal-Cost Scope

Horizontal development cost per lot should include only internal site-development cost: clearing, grading, internal roads, internal stormwater, internal water distribution, internal sewer collection, dry utilities, landscaping/common improvements, testing, and inspections.

Keep external road improvements, water transmission extension, sewer trunk extension, pump stations, special facilities, major off-site mitigation, land acquisition, soft costs, financing, and developer margin separate.

## Formula Conventions

Soft costs: percent of horizontal plus fixed off-site costs.

Contingency: percent of horizontal, fixed off-site, and soft costs.

Developer margin: percent of gross lot-sale revenue.

Selling costs: percent of gross lot-sale revenue.

Financing/carry: simplified percent of horizontal, fixed off-site, soft-cost, and contingency subtotal. No IRR or discounted cash-flow model is proposed for the first portfolio case unless the user requests it in CASE-3B.

## Remaining Missing Inputs

Missing inputs include verified finished-lot comps, builder lot-takedown terms, civil estimate, utility availability and capacity, traffic/off-site scope, entitlement path, approved yield, survey/title/easements, environmental/geotechnical findings, selling terms, financing terms, and acquisition basis.

## User Decisions Required

Approve or revise the cost-scope definition, formula conventions, whether to keep the current lot-value and horizontal-cost ranges for diagnostic modeling, whether to leave other off-site/pump-station allowance blank, selling/carry conventions, and whether CASE-3B should calculate preliminary maximum supportable land price before any acquisition-basis testing.

## Recommendation for CASE-3B

Build the first model as a transparent diagnostic workbook only after user approval. Start with the current assumptions, show the negative residual warning, and use sensitivities to identify what market price, cost, density, and off-site conditions would be required. Do not invent an asking price.
